# Research methodology

## Historical event research

The terminal studies what followed observed historical events over specified horizons. Results should be considered together with sample size, the comparison baseline, assumed direction and costs, and the period covered. A large historical average alone is not sufficient evidence of a usable strategy.

## Chronological boundaries

Chronological evaluations respect the order of observations. Where applicable, split boundaries and embargoes separate fitted or selected research from later evaluation. Pending forward outcomes are excluded from completed-outcome summaries. Final-sample protocols and exposure tracking have a defined scope; they do not prove that data were unseen outside the configured research process.

## Robustness

The lab examines sensitivity across tested settings, chronological partitions, parameter neighborhoods, event overlap/removal, and historical regimes. Connected positive cells are descriptive local support, not independent proof of an optimized plateau.

Historical playback recalculates results across rolling windows. Overlapping windows share observations and should not be treated as independent trials. Display interpolation is distinguished from calculated frames, and sample gaps remain visible.

## Simulation

Portfolio and paper modules use explicit historical timing and cost assumptions. Reconciliation checks verify accounting and lifecycle consistency. A simulated fill is not proof that a comparable live order could have executed. Per-event return statistics, portfolio drawdown, and realized paper-account equity describe different quantities.

## Interpretation

Missing data, unsupported samples, uncertain regime attribution, multiple searches, and weak chronological evidence can change the meaning of a result. The application makes these limitations visible. Historical research and software QA do not establish future returns.

The public showcase uses public-market and non-confidential demonstration material. It contains no private research datasets or trained-model artifacts.
